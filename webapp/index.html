Method & model card

How the numbers are made

  1. Normalise. Grey values are stretched between their 1st and 99th percentile, so detector gain and offset do not matter. The image is optionally resampled to 6 nm/px.
  2. Segment. MillNet (2.5 M parameters) predicts solid, pore and outside-the-layer for every pixel. Besides local texture it sees the whole frame's grey-level distribution and has long-range attention along and through the layer, which is what lifts it above standard U-Nets (+0.02 mIoU, +0.04 pore IoU). It runs in overlapping 512 px tiles.
  3. Measure. Porosity inside the layer, local-thickness pore sizes, chord lengths, interface density, cluster connectivity, and a finite-volume diffusion solve for the tortuosity factor τ = ε / (Deff/D).
  4. Report uncertainty. The porosity range comes from moving the pore probability threshold between 0.35 and 0.65. The Python library adds Monte-Carlo dropout bands.
Annotation correction. The original napari masks turned out to be a raw grey-level threshold inside a hand-drawn layer outline (I < 140–150, changing between annotation sessions). They reported 18 % porosity. The training labels keep the hand-drawn outline, trim the Pt-cap and membrane rim from it, and define pore with one denoised Otsu rule on every frame: 35 % porosity, continuous across frames.
