Four lines of work on the same four-annotator dataset and the same split (train 0–83, validation 87–97, test 101–117). Each asks a different question. This page sets them side by side, shows where they agree, and turns them into one story.

The metrics differ, so absolute numbers are comparable only where the metric matches. Aaditya's report also contains pore IoU in the true layer, the same definition as Dhruv's; it is used below where the two are compared.

1 · Frame statistics: helps known stacks, hurts new ones

all agree
AadityaRemoving frame-statistics conditioning (FiLM) costs 1.7 mIoU and 3–5 points on uncomp. It is the part of MillNet that matters most. But MillNet trained without pristine scores 0.656 on it.
DhruvFiLM adds +0.007 pore IoU on a plain U-Net (3 seeds), so the effect is not MillNet-specific. On unseen pristine FiLM loses the layer itself (layer IoU 0.65) and porosity flips to −10 points.
YunzhiWorks on MillNet with FiLM throughout; on the first unseen fold (comp) the porosity error is 8.3 pp before calibration.

Conditioning on a frame histogram is a strong prior when the histogram looks familiar and an extrapolation when it does not. Dhruv's layer-IoU result is the mechanism behind MillNet's 0.656 on unseen pristine.

2 · Slice context helps the shine-through pores

agree, once measured the same way
Dhruv1 → 7 slices: +0.017 pore IoU (0.851 → 0.868, 3 seeds, spread ≤ 0.001). ±1 slice gives most of it; ±5 is slightly worse.
Hou KinOn synthetic truth the gain sits in the hardest pores (solid one slice below the cut): recall 0.59 → 0.84 on comp and 0.36 → 0.61 on pristine with 9 slices. In v5, 9 slices find 0.73 of them against 0.53 for a single slice, but over-call solid as pore unless corrected.
AadityaIn 3-class mIoU, 1, 3 and 7 slices look the same (0.914 / 0.917 / 0.915 ± 0.004). In pore IoU in the true layer, the same runs give 0.890 (1 slice), 0.900 (3) and 0.898 ± 0.002 (7, 3 seeds): +0.008 from slice context.

Slice context matters for a small set of pixels: pore rims with material just below the cut face. Three-class mIoU is dominated by large solid and outside regions and hides it; pore IoU and Hou's depth-resolved recall show it. The paper should say multi-slice input helps pore detection, measured by pore IoU.

3 · AdaBN rescues pristine and hurts everything else

all agree
DhruvOver 36 leave-one-out models, AdaBN helps unseen pristine in 10 of 12 and hurts comp and uncomp in 24 of 24. A label-free switch on feature-statistics distance does not separate the stacks.
Hou KinThe same split for models trained only on synthetic data: real pristine porosity 0.39 → 0.54, comp and uncomp worse. For v3-trained models, whose training images are already closer, it reverses.
AadityaOn MillNet's cross-stack models AdaBN leaves unseen pristine at 0.656 mIoU and lowers comp and uncomp. Histogram matching helps comp (+4) but hurts pristine.
YunzhiFrozen BatchNorm statistics during fine-tuning is chosen on validation for pristine only; continued BN for uncomp; neither for comp.

Re-normalisation fixes a feature-statistics shift, and only pristine has a large one. Nobody found a label-free rule for when to apply it, so it should not be a default.

5 · Porosity: calibration helps, and the target itself carries error

complementary
YunzhiA decoder threshold chosen on validation, per acquisition, cuts MillNet's porosity MAE 1.50 → 0.84 pp with mIoU 0.930 → 0.932, without retraining. Shared across stacks (joint model, 3 seeds): 2.44 → 1.98 pp, better on comp and uncomp, worse on pristine.
Hou KinOn 13 synthetic stacks a threshold rule tuned to reproduce our start masks is off from truth by −6.3 to +4.1 pp, depending on the material. On see-through rendering it misses up to 18 pp on fibre mats. Its misses line the rim of every pore.
AadityaMillNet-SA2 porosity error on the test block: 0.004–0.024 at the full budget; specific surface and pore connectivity closest to the labels of all models.

Yunzhi's calibration is the cheapest porosity fix and applies directly to MillNet-SA2. Hou Kin's result sets its limit: agreeing with the labels to 1 pp does not mean agreeing with true porosity to 1 pp. Porosity claims in the paper should quote both numbers.

Hou Kin's cross-renderer test shows models do best on the renderer they were trained on: 2.9 pp on their own renderer against 15.6 pp for a model from an older renderer. So MillNet's 0.930 is a matched-renderer result. Scoring each side's models on the other side's data is the cleanest check of any synthetic claim, and the packages are already drop-in compatible.

The combined story

  1. Model. MillNet-SA2 beats nnU-Net on the hand-annotated test set (0.935 vs 0.919 mIoU; in-layer pore IoU 0.919 vs 0.896), mainly through frame-level contrast conditioning. Slice context adds to pore detection (+0.008–0.017 pore IoU; Aaditya, Dhruv) by recovering shallow shine-through (Hou Kin).
  2. Porosity. Add Yunzhi's validation-calibrated decoder: it costs nothing and roughly halves porosity MAE on known stacks.
  3. New acquisitions. Frame conditioning is the weak point (Dhruv, Aaditya). Without labels: mean-teacher on the new stack's unlabelled slices (Dhruv). With labels: 2–5 slices (Aaditya).
  4. Truth. Synthetic data with exact masks shows that both a threshold rule and models carry material-dependent porosity error of several points (Hou Kin). Two independent renderers exist; cross-scoring them is the next step.

Next joint experiments

  1. Score MillNet-SA2 (real-trained and synthetic-trained) on Hou Kin's v5 packages, and Hou Kin's models on the GAN twin.
  2. Apply Yunzhi's decoder calibration to the matched-budget MillNet-SA2 (3 seeds).
  3. Add Dhruv's mean-teacher on unlabelled slices to MillNet training, then rerun the cross-stack test.
  4. Report one shared metric set everywhere: 3-class mIoU, in-layer pore IoU, layer IoU and porosity error.
